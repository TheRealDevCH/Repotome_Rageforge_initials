Technical documentation

How the Mirror Cube works — and why.

A complete breakdown derived from analysing the shipped jars: the reflection algorithm, all fourteen client mixins, the render-pass budget, and the reasoning behind every configuration value.

mirror 0.3.0-rageforge+26.2 rageforge 0.1.0+26.2 Minecraft 26.2 Fabric 0.19+ Java 25+ 14 client mixins

01 Overview

The Mirror Cube is not a shiny texture. It is a real-time planar reflection: for every visible mirror face, the game renders the world a second time from a mirrored camera, into an off-screen buffer, and pastes that image onto the face.

The mod ships as two jars that do very different jobs:

  • mirror — the reflection engine. Blocks, block entity, renderer, framebuffer management, config, and fourteen client-side mixins that bend Minecraft's renderer into rendering a second view.
  • rageforge — a thin content layer. It registers the Rageforge creative tab and the six-sided Spiegelwürfel (Mirror Cube) item/block entry, and declares a hard dependency on mirror.
💡
The one idea behind everything

A flat mirror is optically identical to a window into a mirrored copy of the world. So instead of simulating light bouncing, the mod moves the camera to the mirrored position, renders normally, and clips everything behind the mirror plane away. Every design decision and every performance cost in this mod follows from that single trick.

02 What a mirror really is

When you look at a mirror you do not see the mirror — you see a virtual image of the room behind you, located exactly as far behind the mirror plane as the real object is in front of it.

That means a perfect mirror can be replaced by a hole in the wall with a second camera behind it. The second camera sits at the reflected position of your eye, looks in the reflected direction, and everything it sees is exactly what the mirror should show. This is the entire basis of the technique — no ray tracing, no cube maps, no screen-space approximation.

Consequences of that equivalence

  • Reflections have real parallax. Move sideways and the reflected scene shifts correctly, because you moved a real camera.
  • Reflections are geometrically exact. No distortion at grazing angles, unlike screen-space reflections.
  • Reflections cost a full render. There is no shortcut: seeing a mirror means drawing the world twice.
  • Mirrors can see other mirrors. Which immediately implies recursion, and therefore a need for a hard limit.

03 Planar reflection math

A mirror surface is defined by a point on the plane (its center) and a normal pointing out of the glass. Reflecting the camera across that plane is a few vector operations — and this is literally what the shipped MirrorReflection class does, using Vec3.subtract, dot and scale.

reflect a point across the mirror planeconcept
// d = signed distance from the plane along the normal
double d = point.subtract(center).dot(normal);

// step twice that distance back along the normal
Vec3 mirrored = point.subtract(normal.scale(2.0 * d));

// the view direction reflects the same way, without the offset
Vec3 mirroredDir = dir.subtract(normal.scale(2.0 * dir.dot(normal)));

Two details make this correct rather than merely plausible:

  • Handedness flips. A reflection mirrors space, so triangle winding is inverted. Front faces become back faces; culling has to account for it or the reflected world turns inside out.
  • Only the front half is valid. Geometry behind the mirror must never appear in the reflection. Solving that cleanly is the job of the oblique near plane described below.
🧭
Why a cube needs six of these

Each face of the Mirror Cube is its own plane with its own normal, so each visible face needs its own reflected camera and its own render. That is why a cube is inherently more expensive than the flat wall mirror — and why the mod caps how many mirror surfaces it will draw with maxReflections.

04 Why not just use a texture

Most "mirror" blocks in Minecraft are fakes. It is worth understanding what they give up, because it explains why this mod accepts a heavy performance cost.

ApproachHow it looksCostFails at
Shiny texture Static highlight; nothing actually reflects Free Everything — it is just a decorative block
Cube map / probe Plausible blurry reflection Low No parallax; wrong for flat surfaces; stale when the world changes
Screen-space reflection Good for wet floors Medium Can only reflect what is already on screen; breaks at screen edges
Planar reflection (this mod) Geometrically exact, real parallax, mirrors see mirrors High — a full extra render per face Nothing visually; it is the correct answer, it is just expensive

The mod chooses correctness and hands you the cost dials instead of hiding the trade-off. That is why config/mirror.json exists and why the warning on the front page is prominent.

05 A frame, step by step

What follows is the actual order of operations, reconstructed from the mixin targets and method names in the shipped jar. Each step names the mixin that makes it possible.

The main pass draws the world normally Terrain, entities, sky and clouds are rendered as usual. Mirror faces are drawn as ordinary geometry for now. LevelRendererMainPassMixin · mirror$captureSceneDepth
Scene depth is captured The depth buffer of the finished main pass is copied. It later tells the reflection pass what was already in front of the mirror, so reflections cannot bleed over closer geometry. MirrorFbo · captureSceneDepth · seedDepthInto
Visible mirror surfaces are collected Each mirror block entity contributes its plane (center + normal). The list is sorted and clipped to maxReflections so a wall of mirrors cannot explode the frame. MirrorBlockEntityRenderer · MirrorReflection
The camera is mirrored Position and rotation are reflected across the plane and pushed into Minecraft's camera through an accessor, and view bobbing / hand effects are neutralised so they do not distort the reflected view. CameraInvoker · mirror$setPosition · mirror$setRotation · mirror$setDetached
Rendering is redirected into an off-screen buffer Instead of the screen, the reflection pass writes into a dedicated framebuffer sized by resolutionScale. The captured depth is seeded into it first. GameRendererTargetMixin · mirror$redirectToReflectionFbo
The projection gets an oblique near plane The near clip plane is skewed to coincide with the mirror surface, so anything behind the glass is clipped away for free. This is the trick that keeps reflections physically clean. GameRendererMixin · FrustumMixin · mirror$applyFrustum
Culling and chunk bookkeeping are put in "reflection mode" The frustum offset, the section occlusion octree rebuild and the chunk grid shift are all suppressed during the pass, so the reflected view does not corrupt the main view's culling state. SectionOcclusionGraphMixin · ViewAreaMixin · FrustumMixin
Sky and clouds are guarded Sky and cloud renderers hold their own render targets and state. They are temporarily pointed at the reflection buffer, then restored, so the reflected sky matches the reflected world. SkyRendererMixin · LevelRendererCloudMixin · CloudRendererAccessor
The world is drawn again — recursively if allowed If the reflected view contains another mirror, the whole procedure re-enters, bounded by recursionDepth, nestedPerParent and the global maxRenderPasses budget. GameRendererMixin · mirror$renderReflections
State is restored and the face is textured Camera, projection, frustum, render target and sky state are all rolled back. The finished buffer is sampled as the mirror face's texture, with distance fog applied from fogStartBlocks. MirrorBlockEntityRenderer$State
🔁
Why so much of the code is "save and restore"

Minecraft's renderer assumes exactly one camera and one view per frame. The mod re-enters that renderer while it is already running. Almost every mixin here exists to make a piece of global renderer state temporarily lie about the camera, and then to restore it perfectly — because a single leaked value shows up as flickering, broken culling, or a corrupted sky in the main view.

06 The oblique near plane

A mirrored camera sees too much. It also sees everything on the far side of the mirror — the inside of the wall, the blocks behind the cube — which in reality would be hidden by the glass.

The clean fix is not to sort or mask geometry, but to change the projection: skew the near clip plane so that it lies exactly on the mirror surface. Then the GPU's normal clipping does the work at zero extra cost, and nothing behind the plane can ever be rasterised.

oblique projection, conceptuallyconcept
// The mirror plane is expressed in view space, then the projection
// matrix's third row is rewritten so the near plane coincides with it.
Matrix4f proj = camera.projection();
Vector4f plane = mirrorPlaneInViewSpace();

applyObliqueNearClip(proj, plane);

// From here on, every draw call in the reflection pass is clipped
// against the glass for free — no per-object work required.

The frustum used for culling has to agree with that modified projection, otherwise the game would cull chunks based on the wrong volume. That is exactly what FrustumMixin.mirror$skipOffsetInReflection and LevelExtractorAccessor.mirror$applyFrustum exist for: they let the mod install its own frustum for the duration of the pass.

⚠
Side effect worth knowing

An oblique near plane makes depth precision non-uniform. In practice this can show up as subtle z-fighting on surfaces that sit almost exactly in the mirror plane — which is one reason the mirror block itself is a full block with the reflective surface slightly inset rather than an infinitely thin sheet.

07 Framebuffers and depth seeding

Reflections cannot be drawn straight to the screen — they have to land in a texture. MirrorFbo owns that: it allocates render targets, keeps a pool of them (maxLevels for the recursion depth), seeds depth into them, and releases them all when the pass is over.

MirrorFbo — observed membersfrom the shipped class
class MirrorFbo {
    TextureTarget level(int index, int width, int height);  // per-recursion target
    void          seedDepthInto(TextureTarget target);           // reuse main-pass depth
    void          captureSceneDepth();                        // snapshot the main pass
    int           maxLevels;                                    // bounded by recursionDepth
    void          releaseAll();                               // hand buffers back
}

Why depth is seeded rather than cleared

If the reflection buffer started with an empty depth buffer, the reflected image would happily draw over things that are actually in front of the mirror in the real view — a player standing between you and the cube, for instance. By copying the main pass's depth into the reflection target first, the reflection is automatically occluded by whatever was already closer.

Why buffers are pooled

Each recursion level needs its own target, because level n is still being written while level n+1 renders into a different one. Allocating those per frame would thrash GPU memory, so they are created once per size and reused, then released together.

08 Recursion and the render budget

Two mirrors facing each other are mathematically an infinite series of reflections. Something has to stop it, and the mod uses three independent limits that all guard different failure modes.

LimitGuards againstShape of the growth it tames
maxReflections Many mirrors visible at once Linear in the number of mirror faces on screen
recursionDepth Mirror-in-mirror chains Depth of the reflection tree
nestedPerParent One reflection spawning many children Branching factor — this is the exponential one
maxRenderPasses Everything above combining badly Absolute ceiling on total passes per frame

The first three describe a tree; the fourth is a hard stop on its total size. With the shipped defaults the theoretical worst case is roughly 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 = 15 passes along one chain (nestedPerParent = 2, recursionDepth = 4), while maxRenderPasses = 48 caps the whole frame regardless of how the tree is shaped.

🌳
Why four knobs instead of one

A single "quality" slider would either be too coarse or too dangerous. These four separate the cases that actually behave differently: a hall of mirrors (breadth), a pair of facing mirrors (depth), a nested arrangement (branching), and the raw frame budget. You can allow deep recursion on one cube while still refusing to render twenty of them.

09 Why you are invisible in it

This is the mod's signature quirk and it follows directly from how the reflected camera is set up.

In first person, Minecraft does not render your own player model — the camera is inside it, and the game only draws the held item and hand. The reflection pass reuses that same camera machinery via CameraInvoker, including mirror$setDetached, and the hand/item rendering is explicitly handled (GameRendererMixin.mirror$seedHandDepth, which brackets renderItemInHand) and GameRendererAccessor neutralises bobView, bobHurt and the spinning-effect timers.

The consequence: the reflection is rendered from a camera that behaves like your camera, and your camera has never drawn your body. So the world, mobs, blocks and even other players appear in the mirror — and you do not.

👤
Bug or feature?

Physically it is wrong; a real mirror shows you. In practice it is the cheapest possible behaviour and it is stable, so the mod embraces it. It is also the single most recognisable trait of the block — a mirror that reflects your entire world but refuses to reflect you.

Two details in that tree are worth calling out. First, the presence of mirror_project.vsh / .fsh: a dedicated shader program that samples the reflection buffer using projected coordinates, which is how a flat texture ends up aligned with the world instead of sliding across the face. Second, mirror_bar_* and mirror_corner_* models — the frame pieces of the original flat mirror. The Rageforge cube deliberately has none of them, which is what makes it borderless.

Reading the pattern

Notice that exactly five of the fourteen do real work (renderReflections, redirectToReflectionFbo, captureSceneDepth, applyFrustum, seedHandDepth). The other nine are accessors and guards. That ratio is the honest summary of this mod: the reflection idea is simple, and almost all of the engineering is about re-entering a renderer that was never designed to be re-entered.

13 Blocks and registration

The flat mirror

MirrorBlock extends HorizontalDirectionalBlock. On placement it reads getHorizontalDirection().getOpposite() so the reflective side faces the player, and it consults Level.getFluidState at the clicked position — standard behaviour for a block that can be waterlogged or placed in fluid. Its models include mirror_glass plus four mirror_bar_* and four mirror_corner_* pieces, which is how it gets a visible frame.

The Mirror Cube

MirrorCubeBlock extends Block — deliberately not directional, because a cube with six reflective faces has no meaningful facing. It declares a MapCodec via simpleCodec, creates a MirrorBlockEntity, and returns a RenderShape that hands drawing over to the block entity renderer instead of the normal block model path.

🧊
Why the cube is borderless

The flat mirror ships frame geometry; the cube does not reference any of it. Its blockstate points at a single full-cube model whose faces are entirely reflective surface. Visually that is the whole point of the Rageforge variant — an object with no frame, no edge trim and no obvious "front", which is exactly what the site's logo depicts.

14 The Rageforge layer

  • Builds a CreativeModeTab using the builder API: title(Component), an icon Supplier, and a DisplayItemsGenerator.

15 The cost model

Rendering cost scales with the number of extra passes times the cost of each pass. The config splits neatly along that line: four values limit how many passes happen, three limit what each pass costs.

rough mental modelconcept
frameCost  ≈  mainPass  +  Σ (passCost)  over all reflection passes

passes     ≤  min( maxRenderPasses,
                   tree( maxReflections, recursionDepth, nestedPerParent ) )

passCost   ∝  resolutionScale²                // pixels — quadratic!
            ×  visibleGeometry( reflectionDistanceChunks, fogStartBlocks )

Why resolutionScale dominates

It is the only value that enters quadratically. Going from 1.0 to 0.7 is not a 30% saving — it is roughly 0.7² = 0.49, so about half the pixels per pass, across every pass simultaneously. Nothing else in the config comes close as a single change.

Why nestedPerParent is the dangerous one

It is the branching factor of the reflection tree. With nestedPerParent = 3 and recursionDepth = 4 the worst case is 1+3+9+27 = 40 passes on a single chain; raise it to 4 and it becomes 85. This is why maxRenderPasses exists as a separate absolute ceiling — it is the value that saves you when the other three multiply.

Where the cost actually lands

  • GPU fill rate — driven by resolutionScale and pass count.
  • CPU draw-call submission — each pass re-submits terrain and entities; reflectionDistanceChunks is the main lever.
  • GPU memory — one render target per recursion level at the scaled resolution.
  • Culling work — a frustum per pass, which is why so much mixin effort goes into not rebuilding occlusion data.

nestedPerParent2 How many child reflections one reflection may spawn. Exponential — branching factor

⚠
Sanity rule

Keep reflectionDistanceChunks at or below your normal render distance. A reflection that reaches further than your own view is paying for chunks you would not otherwise draw at all.

⚠
Note on the showcase preset

nestedPerParent stays at 2 on purpose. Raising depth is comparatively safe; raising the branching factor is what produces frame-time spikes. If you want deeper infinite mirrors, push recursionDepth, not nestedPerParent.

18 Why Sodium works

Sodium replaces Minecraft's terrain renderer with its own. That would normally break a mod that re-enters the render pipeline — but the mod's own metadata explains the approach, and it is worth quoting because it is the cleanest possible summary:

📋
From fabric.mod.json, key mirror:notes

"Compatible with Sodium: during a reflection the mod points Sodium's terrain projection at the mirror's oblique projection so Sodium draws the reflected view (not the scene behind the mirror), then restores it."

The key insight: the mod does not fight Sodium or bypass it. It lets Sodium do the terrain drawing it is good at, and only changes the projection matrix Sodium renders with. Sodium is drawing the reflected view without knowing anything about mirrors — it simply obeys the matrix it is given, and the matrix is restored afterwards.

19 Why Iris does not

Iris is listed under breaks in fabric.mod.json, which means Fabric Loader will refuse to run them together rather than let you discover the breakage in-game.

✕
The structural conflict

Iris substitutes the entire render pipeline with a shader-pack-driven one: its own passes, its own targets, its own deferred stages. The reflection pass works by re-entering Minecraft's pipeline mid-frame with a modified camera and projection. Under Iris that pipeline no longer exists in the form the mod re-enters, so there is nothing to re-enter.

This is not a bug that can be patched around — it is two mods claiming ownership of the same stage of the frame.

Sodium alone is supported and recommended. Sodium plus Iris is not.

Shaders

assets/mirror/shaders/core/mirror_project.vsh and .fsh implement the projected sampling of the reflection buffer. The vertex stage computes the projected texture coordinate for each fragment of the mirror face; the fragment stage samples the reflection target and applies distance fog. This is what makes the reflection stay locked to the world as you move, instead of behaving like a decal stuck to the block.

24 Free forever

This mod is free software and it must stay that way. It was built for the community, on top of an MIT-licensed engine, and there is no version of it that costs money.

The reflection engine is MIT licensed, which permits commercial use in the legal sense. That is not the point. The intent of this project is explicit: the Mirror Cube is not to be sold. If you find it behind a payment, a paywall, a premium tier or a paid modpack, that distribution is not sanctioned by the project.

Encouraged

  • Download and play it — for anyone, on any server, indefinitely.
  • Put it in a modpack — as long as the pack itself is free to obtain.
  • Read and modify the code — the engine is MIT; learning from it is the point.
  • Stream and record it — monetised videos included, no permission needed.
  • Redistribute it — with license files intact and credit to the upstream project.

Not permitted

  • Selling the mod — no paid downloads, no premium builds, no paid early access.
  • Paywalling access — no subscription, Patreon or donation gate required to obtain it.
  • Paid modpacks — do not bundle it into a pack that charges for access.
  • Pay-per-download reuploads — no ad-gates, link shorteners or reward-per-download sites.
  • Stripping credits — do not remove the MIT license or present the engine as your own work.
✕
In one sentence
